# Crossbar and DRN experimental results for the meeting on 22 October 2026

Evidence updated through **19 October 2026**. This document brings together the crossbar corruption sweeps, noiseless/noisy crossbar learning, DRN HWA and calibration controls, the completed DRN recovery LR sweep, and the direct comparison. Lower teacher KL and perplexity (PPL) are better; the two metrics need not move together.

**Crossbars:** intelligent P&V supplies most of the recovery in the tested CMO regime. At 6% corruption, WikiText PPL falls from about 200 to 51.6. Noiseless learning improves every case in the completed one-array sweep; noisy learning adds smaller, regime-dependent gains. **DRNs:** tuned recovery at random 3% corruption reduces WikiText KL by about 61%, but recovered DRNs still have higher KL and PPL than the closest crossbar results. Their training budgets and write rules differ, so this is a descriptive comparison.

Sections 1–4 retain the original 15 October crossbar evidence and costs. Section 5 adds the complete 16 October noiseless/noisy crossbar sweep, superseding the earlier learning-coverage gaps. Sections 6–7 add the DRN results and crossbar comparison reviewed on 19 October.

### What each stage does

1. **Initial P&V:** deploy the frozen HWA weights with the CMO 2% acceptance endpoint fit. Permanent faults remain stuck. The 2% acceptance setting describes programming precision, not the corruption rate.
2. **Intelligent P&V:** read both devices of each weight, identify deviations with a 5σ detector, and program a healthy partner to compensate the stuck device. Either the active or reference device can be programmed. The logical targets remain frozen; out-of-range partner targets are clipped. This is fault-aware pair rewriting, with no learning.
3. **P&V then learning:** start from that rewritten array and train the original active devices with accumulated Adam commands, a nominal 2σ write threshold, LR 0.003 and at most four write attempts per active device. The budget is 256 updates / 2,097,152 adaptation tokens. Digital parameters and reference devices stay fixed during learning. The objective is teacher KL and the final checkpoint is fixed in advance.

The device encoding is q = active − reference, with gmin ≈ 9 µS and gmax ≈ 88.2 µS. The faults are permanent and synthetic, with exact totals per MLP and no extra die/array rate variation in this sweep. Read noise and drift are disabled. Results use held apparent states and are exploratory simulations.

### Reading the PPL result

- **gmin:** learning lowers WikiText PPL at all three measured rates, by 0.541, 0.338 and 0.245 points at 2%, 4% and 6%.
- **gmax:** learning is almost neutral at 2% (+0.004 PPL), worsens PPL at 4% (+0.189), and improves it at 6% (−0.523).
- **PPL and teacher KL differ.** At gmax 4%, teacher KL improves from 0.082736 to 0.076775 while PPL worsens. The growing fraction of residual KL removed in the experiment report must not be presented as a growing PPL improvement.
- Learning after rewriting has one-array coverage here. Digital calibration was not tested in this sweep, so these results do not establish that learning is the best correction at matched cost.

## 5. Noiseless versus noisy on-chip learning (added 16 October)

Every case above now has two learning results, both starting from the intelligent-P&V state of the same array (271002):

- **Noisy learning** is the recipe of section 1, unchanged: thresholded Adam with a nominal 2σ write threshold, constant LR 0.003, at most four attempts per active device, and physical write noise.
- **Noiseless learning** writes every Adam update exactly into every healthy active device, with a cosine schedule peaking at 0.003. It is a non-physical bound on what the same objective, data and budget could reach. Its schedule was chosen on development data in an earlier screen ([exp-021](../../hwa-exp021/campaigns/opt-cmo-corruption/series/002-onchip-recovery/results/exp-021.md)); with exact writes, a constant 0.003 harms the healthy array.

All values in this section come from one matched run on V100 GPUs. Initial-P&V values equal those above. Intelligent-P&V and noisy-learning values differ from sections 1 and 3 by up to 0.06 WikiText PPL, because the rewrite's programming draws differ by GPU.

**Noiseless learning improves every case.** It lowers WikiText PPL by 0.68–1.32 and removes 53–80% of the excess teacher KL above healthy deployment left after intelligent P&V. It also improves the healthy array. **Noisy learning helps mainly at 4–6%,** removing 15–32% of that KL. It is neutral or harmful at 1–2%, on random 3% and on the healthy array. This is consistent with a limitation from noisy writes and gating, but the two laws also differ in schedule and write cap, so the comparison does not isolate write noise alone.

## 6. DRN HWA and recovery results (added 19 October)

The DRN experiments use OPT-125M with the same six analog MLP positions, layers 6–11, implemented as **perfect-diode dissipative resistive networks**. There are 28,311,552 logical weights and **113,246,208 physical devices**, four per logical weight. Every device-facing forward, gradient, selection score and final evaluation uses the current held apparent state. The CMO model is a hardware-derived fit with synthetic permanent faults; these are simulations, not measured hardware training.

### Earlier HWA and digital-calibration controls

Both off-chip arms start from the same DRN mapping of the pinned std3 digital master. “Clean” below disables noise during the DRN adaptation phase; it is not a never-HWA-trained teacher. Each arm ran 30,505 updates. The selected checkpoints are at update 1,536 for clean and 30,505 for HWA, so these are selected-model comparisons, not equal-step comparisons.

### New bounded recovery: complete learning-rate sweep at 3%

All 12 candidates restart from the **same HWA + P&V + partner-compensated random-3% deployment**, assignment 271002. Each receives **2,048 updates, effective batch 32, sequence length 256 and 16,777,216 input-token presentations**. Digital parameters stay fixed. Adam uses a constant LR; both arms have **unlimited device-write attempts**.

Before writing, the proposed target is clipped to **[9, 88.199997] µS**. Noiseless recovery applies every nonzero command except projected no-ops. Noisy recovery accumulates signed commands and issues a write only when the command magnitude is at least **2ρ**, where **ρ is the write-noise standard deviation at the bounded proposed target, evaluated before writing**. The gate acts on the accumulated command before target projection, so clipping can make the actual target displacement smaller than 2ρ. The noisy endpoint is sampled once per write and then held; it is not clipped after noise. Unwritten cells retain their state, and permanent defects remain stuck.

Select one LR per arm by the **lowest fixed-final development KL**, then evaluate the locked endpoints on held-out data. All 12 candidates completed and passed native validation; bold marks the selected LRs. The unchanged development KL is **0.12147519**.

| LR | Noiseless development KL | Noisy development KL | Noiseless write attempts | Noisy write attempts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1e-5 | 0.04898595 | 0.06708378 | 222,045,354,704 | 7,800,531 |
| 3e-5 | 0.04080222 | 0.05154769 | 223,556,121,410 | 22,167,760 |
| 1e-4 | **0.03496502** | 0.03889131 | 224,152,665,876 | 70,161,815 |
| 3e-4 | 0.03987184 | **0.03573988** | 224,453,261,211 | 256,210,235 |
| 1e-3 | 0.06877336 | 0.08254629 | 224,192,370,004 | 2,065,252,671 |
| 3e-3 | 0.12778207 | 0.15594310 | 223,308,997,583 | 7,109,662,261 |

The winners are **1e-4 noiseless** and **3e-4 noisy**; both are interior points of the grid. LR 3e-3 worsens development KL in both arms. Intermediate checkpoints and held-out scores were not used to choose the winners.

The closest available crossbar comparison is exp-022 from section 5: the same OPT-125M model family, six analog MLP positions and 3% random physical-device faults. Both comparisons start learning after HWA, P&V and partner compensation. Both evaluate held apparent states, with 256 development, 384 generated and 1,024 WikiText sequences of length 256. These are **separate architecture-specific HWA and deployment states**, not one shared physical state.

After learning, DRN WikiText KL is about **52% higher for noiseless** and **29% higher for noisy** recovery; its perplexity is roughly **3% higher** in each case.

### Differences that limit the architecture comparison

| Setting | Crossbar, exp-022 | DRN, bounded-recovery pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recovery updates per candidate | 256 | 2,048 |
| Effective batch / input-token presentations | 32 / 2,097,152 | 32 / 16,777,216 |
| Physical devices | 56,623,104: active + reference | 113,246,208: four per logical weight |
| Devices eligible for learning | 28,311,552 active cells; references fixed | All four conductance arrays, subject to permanent defects |
| Noiseless LR | Cosine schedule, peak 3e-3, 13 warm-up updates | Constant 1e-4, selected in this pilot |
| Noisy LR | Constant 3e-3; not retuned after partner rewriting | Constant 3e-4, selected in this pilot |
| Noisy minimum command | Accumulated, fixed nominal 2σ | Accumulated, target-dependent 2ρ before writing |
| Write-attempt cap | Noiseless: none; noisy: four per active cell | None for either law |

The DRN receives **eight times as many training updates**. Its write-target clipping is also part of the new recovery rule. The HWA checkpoints and topology differ; the same assignment seed does not make their fault maps identical. Different GPUs and write RNG realizations further limit exact matching. These endpoints therefore describe the tested systems; they do not isolate architecture as the cause of the gap.

For additional cost context, crossbar noisy recovery makes **36,793,504 attempts / 35,658,426 healthy writes**, versus **256,210,235 / 254,801,368** for the DRN. Crossbar noiseless recovery records **6,988,020,558 healthy write events**, versus **217,217,601,999** for the DRN. The different update horizons, write caps, thresholds and numbers of trainable cells prevent a matched write-efficiency conclusion.

The current evidence supports useful recovery of this DRN deployment, with a remaining crossbar gap. A controlled architecture comparison would require a declared matched tuning and training budget, an uncapped noisy crossbar arm and the same target-dependent 2ρ write rule. ## 8. DRN recovery at other corruption levels — accepted endpoints

**Completed evaluation: 20 October.** All ten saved endpoints at 1%, 2%, 4%,
5% and 6% random corruption have been evaluated on development, generated
held-out and WikiText held-out data. The user accepted the available
**1,954–2,002-update** checkpoints after the original 2,048-update runs stopped
at their wall-time boundaries and the storage reservation blocked continuation.
Actual counts are shown below. This is a retrospective horizon amendment;
the original training attempts remain recorded as interrupted.

Both laws keep the LRs selected at 3%: **1e-4 noiseless / 3e-4 noisy**.
Each rate uses one shared HWA + P&V + partner-compensated deployment, seed
271002. Effective batch 32, microbatch 1, held apparent-state forwards, bounded
write targets and unlimited writes are unchanged. Noisy writes retain the
pre-write 2ρ command threshold described in section 6. Evaluation performs
no device writes. The previous 3% result remains an RTX 5090 reference;
these five rates ran on V100s with PyTorch 2.5.0.

### Reading the recovery results

The LRs selected at 3% transfer well to the other tested corruption levels.
Both recovery policies lower development, generated-text and WikiText KL
relative to their matched HWA + P&V + partner-compensated starting states.
The noisy 2ρ policy achieves comparable or better recovery with roughly three
orders of magnitude fewer write attempts. These are model-based results for
one assignment per rate, not fabricated-hardware measurements.

The compact WikiText comparison below includes the earlier 3% reference.
Lower PPL is better; bold marks the better of the two recovery policies.
The final column is the fraction of matched hold KL removed by noisy recovery.

| Corruption | Before recovery PPL | Noiseless PPL | Noisy PPL | WikiText KL removed, noisy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1% | 49.418 | **49.192** | 49.440 | 35.1% |
| 2% | 50.880 | **49.757** | 49.958 | 52.2% |
| 3%* | 53.779 | **50.576** | 51.032 | 60.8% |
| 4% | 58.095 | 52.346 | **52.008** | 66.7% |
| 5% | 63.621 | 54.076 | **53.643** | 69.2% |
| 6% | 70.890 | 56.301 | **55.395** | 69.0% |

*The 3% point is the earlier RTX 5090 result at 2,048 updates (section 6).
The other rates are V100 runs at 1,954–2,002 updates. Different GPU/PyTorch
environments and programming draws prevent treating this as an exact
cross-host replication.

- **At low corruption, the PPL gain is smaller.** At 1%, noiseless recovery
  changes WikiText PPL from 49.418 to 49.192; noisy recovery slightly worsens
  it to 49.440 despite improving KL. KL measures agreement with the teacher,
  whereas PPL measures prediction of the actual next tokens. They can move
  differently.
- **At 4–6%, recovery gives substantial gains.** At 6%, noisy recovery takes
  WikiText PPL from 70.89 to 55.39 and removes 69% of the starting KL.
  Its remaining error is still higher than at low corruption: recovery
  compensates for much of the damage without eliminating it.
- **The noisy policy overtakes noiseless at higher corruption.** Its WikiText
  KL is 2.9%, 7.4% and 9.9% lower at 4%, 5% and 6%, respectively. The same
  ordering appears on development data.

**Interpretation:** the thresholded noisy policy offers a promising accuracy
and write-cost tradeoff, particularly at higher corruption. Its advantage
cannot be attributed to noise itself: the arms also differ in LR and update
rule. Independent assignments and a matched comparison of update rules would
be needed to establish the mechanism. These results show benefit for the
observed deployments rather than a general necessity for on-chip learning.

The scale of the write-cost difference is easier to see per run and per device:

| Across the new V100 runs | Noiseless | Noisy 2ρ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Total write attempts per run | 211–221 billion | 227–283 million |
| Average attempts per physical device over the whole training run | 1,866–1,955 | 2.0–2.5 |

These averages cover all 113,246,208 physical devices, including stuck cells;
they are not caps, and individual cells can receive different numbers of
attempts. The noisy policy accumulates signed commands until they pass the
pre-write 2ρ threshold. Noiseless recovery applies nonzero commands except
projected no-ops. Actual target displacement can be smaller than 2ρ after
clipping, as described in section 6.

The noisy policy uses **747–974 times fewer write attempts**
than the corresponding noiseless policy. This compares whole update
policies, including different LRs and the 2ρ accumulation gate; it does
not isolate the effect of noise. These modeled events are not measured
pulse counts, energy or hardware latency.

Each corruption level uses one assignment seed, and the accepted update
counts differ slightly between arms. The results demonstrate recovery
benefit for these deployments; they do not establish replication,
a pure noise advantage, or that on-chip learning is necessary.

both training waves, old canaries and endpoint evaluation. All ten
evaluation jobs completed with exit 0:0. Twenty accepted/baseline
checkpoints were collected; their hashes and 25 completed native
bundles were verified locally. All original remote checkpoints remain
preserved.
