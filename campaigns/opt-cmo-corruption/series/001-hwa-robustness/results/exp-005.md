## Training-duration interpretation

The user asked whether the current training budget is too short. Each full
trial exposes 104,857,600 tokens: 12,800 updates of 32 sequences of length 256.
Against the 195,226-sequence synthetic corpus, this is about 2.098 corpus
passes. The configured 50 epochs are runner intervals of 8,192 sequences each,
not 50 passes through the corpus.

Seven of the first eight candidates selected update 12,800. During the final
1,024 updates, step 1e-3 improved mean development KL from 0.469284 to
0.458397 (2.32%), and step 5e-3 improved from 0.444184 to 0.429946 (3.21%).
These curves leave convergence unresolved. The step schedule has only 2,560
updates after its tenfold LR reduction; the linear schedule reaches zero LR
at the endpoint, so a flattening tail alone cannot establish convergence.

The current comparison ranks learning rates at a fixed training budget. A
larger LR may reach useful configurations sooner; these results do not show
that it remains preferable after longer training, or that extra epochs would
remove its healthy-performance cost. A useful next comparison would test
roughly four corpus passes at moderate and high LR before extending the LR
boundary further. Extending the existing post-decay tail and stretching the
entire schedule are different interventions and must be declared separately.
Native selected-source artifacts do not contain the optimizer/RNG state needed
for an exact continuation. No duration extension has been launched or added to
the queue; the authorized 1e-2 trials continue under their existing protocol.
