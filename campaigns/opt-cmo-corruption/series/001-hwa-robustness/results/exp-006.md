## Interpretation and next decision

Small digital updates help corruption-aware HWA as well as standard HWA in this
one-seed experiment. Digital training is not inherently detrimental: 1e-5
improves both development components and all matched test conditions over the
analog counterpart, while 1e-4 is worse on development. This comparison does not
identify which digital parameter groups cause the effect.

The incremental benefit at 3% faults is modest and below the frozen material
threshold. Corruption-aware HWA remains useful at higher faults, with a healthy
penalty. At 3%, its WikiText advantage over standard-9% analog is only 2.97%;
the 19.84% gain over standard 3% must not be presented as a gain over every
existing control. No recipe dominates all rates and domains.

This is exploratory, model-based evidence from one training seed and adaptively
reused test cohorts/arrays. Five assignments are not five training replicates,
and matched assignments across rates are not independent across rates. Late
checkpoint selection alone does not show that more epochs would improve transfer.
No on-chip writes, physical update costs or fabricated-hardware results were
measured.

Retain standard 3% and corruption-aware 3%, each with digital peak 1e-5, as
candidates for the previously discussed independent-training-seed confirmation,
preserving joint healthy/faulty selection and the standard-9% reference. This
phase is complete. Additional seeds were not dispatched; that later phase needs
its own cases and budget before execution.
