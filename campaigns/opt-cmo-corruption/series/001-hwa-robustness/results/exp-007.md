## Frozen comparisons and interpretation

The material-effect rule requires at least 4/5 array wins and at least 10% mean
paired relative KL reduction. It is a predeclared practical criterion, not a
training-seed significance test. In every one of the 72 comparisons, all five
arrays agree on the sign; meeting the magnitude threshold remains a separate
condition.

**Explicit fault modelling helps beyond standard 3% Gaussian HWA in defective
regimes.** For the 3%-fault-aware recipe, the material rule passes on WikiText at
3–5% actual faults and generated text at 2–5%. The healthy-chip cost remains:
healthy WikiText perplexity rises from 49.512 to 53.971 and generated perplexity
from 7.225 to 7.490. The 5%-training recipe passes against standard 3% Gaussian
on WikiText at 4–5% and generated text at 2–5%, with a larger healthy penalty.

**The matched 9% Gaussian control changes the conclusion at moderate corruption.**
It has the lowest WikiText KL and perplexity at 2% and 3% actual faults. At 3%,
its WikiText KL is 0.147380 versus 0.151854 for 3%-fault-aware HWA; on generated
text the order reverses (0.087784 versus 0.079544). Thus explicit masks do not
provide a material improvement over stronger Gaussian noise in either domain
at 3% under the frozen rule. The earlier analog-only 9% reference was not this
matched comparison and should not substitute for it.

For the 3%-fault-aware recipe relative to the new 9% Gaussian control, positive
values below favor explicit faults. These are paired relative changes, not
ratios of the reported means:

| Actual faults | WikiText paired KL reduction / wins | Generated paired KL reduction / wins |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0% | -60.97% / 0/5 | -66.47% / 0/5 |
| 1% | -32.50% / 0/5 | -23.78% / 0/5 |
| 2% | -15.54% / 0/5 | -3.18% / 0/5 |
| 3% | -3.04% / 0/5 | 9.39% / 5/5 |
| 4% | 5.63% / 5/5 | 17.30% / 5/5 **passes** |
| 5% | 11.62% / 5/5 **passes** | 22.74% / 5/5 **passes** |

At 5% actual faults, explicit 3% training therefore supplies a material gain in
both domains: **11.62% WikiText and 22.74% generated KL reduction, each 5/5 wins**.
At 4%, it supplies a material generated-text gain (17.30%), while the WikiText
improvement (5.63%) is below the threshold. Stronger Gaussian noise already
materially improves on standard 3% Gaussian in WikiText at 2–5% and generated
text at 1–5%, but costs healthy performance.

**Training with 5% temporary faults does not justify replacing 3% in this regime.**
It is worse on WikiText at every actual fault rate, in all five arrays. On
generated text it is worse through 4%; at 5% its KL improves only 1.99%, below
the material threshold, while perplexity slightly worsens (7.838 versus 7.833).
Its healthy WikiText perplexity is 57.086 versus 53.971 for the 3% recipe. Its
flatter corruption curve therefore does not by itself establish better absolute
performance. Relative to 9% Gaussian, its generated-text gains pass at 4–5%,
while its WikiText gains pass at no tested rate.

## Decision and limits

This phase is complete and reviewed. Retain standard 3% Gaussian as the
healthy-chip reference, matched 9% Gaussian as a strong moderate-corruption
control, and 3%-fault-aware HWA as the higher-corruption control. The current
results provide no material reason to promote the 5%-training source over 3%
within 0–5% actual faults. No additional experiment is launched by this decision.

These are exploratory, model-based results from one training seed, adaptively
reused test cohorts and arrays, synthetic fault rates, and a common frozen LR
rather than separate per-recipe optimization. Array consistency does not establish
training-seed reproducibility. Late selection leaves longer-training behavior
unresolved. The healthy/3% selection rule does not test optimally selected 5%
specialists, and no conclusion covers actual fault rates above 5%. The experiment
does not measure on-chip recovery, physical write costs, drift, spatially clustered
faults or fabricated-hardware performance.
