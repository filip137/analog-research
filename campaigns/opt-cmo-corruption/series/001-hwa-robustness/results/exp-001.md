**The frozen rule, per size.** A source beats another at a rate when it has
lower KL on at least 4 of 5 arrays and a mean relative KL reduction of at
least 10 %.

- **HWA vs pretrained, 1–5 %:** both HWA sources beat the pretrained model at
  every rate, size and array (5/5).
  - L3: 85.8–92.6 % lower KL (standard), 82.0–95.9 % (corruption-aware).
  - L6: 89.3–94.7 % (standard), 86.2–96.3 % (corruption-aware).
- **Fidelity at 0 %:** the pretrained model beats both HWA sources, and
  standard beats corruption-aware.
  - L3: pretrained over standard +28.7 %, over corruption-aware +71.8 %;
    standard over corruption-aware +60.4 %.
  - L6: pretrained over standard +44.4 %, over corruption-aware +71.4 %;
    standard over corruption-aware +48.5 %.
- **Standard vs corruption-aware under stuck devices:** a crossover.
  - L3: standard wins at 1 % (5/5, +20.7 %); corruption-aware wins at 2, 3, 4
    and 5 % (5/5; +11.4, +28.5, +39.1 and +44.8 %).
  - L6: standard wins at 1 % (5/5, +22.7 %); at 2 % neither wins (3/5,
    +1.1 %); corruption-aware wins at 3, 4 and 5 % (5/5; +18.4, +30.6 and
    +38.3 %).
- **Destructive rate:** none. The rule needs every source above 2× teacher
  perplexity, but both HWA sources stay at or below 1.25× (L6 standard at
  5 %). The pretrained source alone exceeds 2× from 3 % in L3 and from 1 % in
  L6.
- **Carry-forward:** the rule as written returns an empty set. The pretrained
  model is beaten everywhere, and each HWA source is beaten by the other at
  some rate.

**What the trajectories show.**
- **Corruption-aware sources have plateaued at this rate.** Their healthy
  component is flat. Epoch-to-epoch jitter is about ±1.5 % for L6, so part
  of the plateau may be the noise floor of a constant learning rate.
- **Standard HWA has not plateaued.** Its robustness to stuck devices still
  improves by about 9 % per 10 epochs, even though it never trains with
  stuck devices.
- **The development/training ratio is suggestive only.** It compares unlike
  conditions:
  - training loss uses sampled noise on the training split;
  - development KL uses real P&V on two fixed arrays on the validation split.

  The ratio grows in all four sources, which suggests the beginning of
  overfitting to the 2.1 M-token WikiText-2 training cohort. This is
  strongest in L6, where the healthy development KL of standard HWA barely
  moves (0.061 → 0.051) while its training loss falls by 38 %. It has not
  been checked under matched conditions (the same arrays on a training
  subset).

## Interpretation

**Observations.**
- **HWA dominates.** Without HWA, OPT-125M with CMO MLPs is unusable beyond
  about 1 % stuck devices (L6) or 2 % (L3).
- **Both HWA sources stay close to the teacher.** At 5 % stuck devices
  perplexity stays within 13 % of the teacher (L3 standard) or 6 % (L3
  corruption-aware), and within 24 % and 10 % for L6.
- **The two HWA arms trade off.** Training with temporary stuck devices buys
  roughly a 2× lower KL at 5 % stuck devices. It costs about 2.5× higher KL on
  healthy hardware: 0.045 vs 0.018 in L3, still only 1.03× the teacher's
  perplexity.
- **Arrays barely matter.** The spread across the 5 independent arrays is
  tiny for both HWA sources: the max/min KL ratio is at most about 1.04. So
  the 4-of-5 criterion is decisive wherever the mean difference is material.

**Inference and limits.**
- **"Standard HWA" is robustness-tuned.** Its rate (5e-3) was chosen by a
  score dominated by the 3 % case, so the standard-vs-corruption-aware contrast
  isolates only the corruption masks, not selection. A clean-selected standard
  HWA would probably be more faithful at 0 % and less robust above.
- **The budget favours corruption-aware HWA.** The corruption-aware sources
  had plateaued at 50 epochs, while standard HWA was still gaining
  robustness. More training would likely narrow corruption-aware HWA's
  advantage at 2–5 % and move the crossover up. So the location of the
  crossover is conditional on this budget.
  - Its cost on healthy hardware (about 2.5× standard's KL) sits on a flat
    curve, so it is not an artefact of under-training.
  - With a growing development/training ratio, longer training on the same
    2.1 M tokens may buy less than the fit suggests.
- **The training data are narrow.** HWA trained for 50 passes over
  WikiText-2's 2.1 M training tokens, and the test set is also WikiText-2.
  The test KL therefore cannot reveal domain specialization of the analog
  MLPs. Analog Foundation Models instead distils on 20 B tokens sampled from
  the model itself, and LLM-QAT also uses data generated by the model.
- **One training run per source.** The array spread does not measure
  training-seed variance.
- **The regime is narrow.** Independent placement only, random stuck values
  only, one training corruption rate (3 %), synthetic stuck statistics.
- **The two L6 sources trained in different environments,** a recorded
  deviation that is expected to matter little.
- **Evidence class:** exploratory and model-based, not hardware measurements.

## Decision

**Settled:**
- Drop the pretrained model as a deployment source for on-chip recovery.
- No stuck rate in 0–5 % is destructive for the HWA sources, so recovery
  experiments can use the whole range.

**Pending the user.** The carry-forward rule returns no source because of the
crossover. Proposed for Experiment 2:
- carry both HWA sources forward, since each is the best no-weight-learning
  deployment in its own range;
- test on-chip recovery at one rate where standard HWA wins (1 %) and at
  least one where corruption-aware HWA wins (3 or 5 %), at both sizes.

In that range the best HWA source still leaves KL of 0.04–0.15 (L3) and
0.08–0.15 (L6) above the teacher. Calibration, rewrite and on-chip learning
must close that gap to be viable.
