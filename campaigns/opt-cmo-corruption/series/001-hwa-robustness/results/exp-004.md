**Frozen rule:** the same source type, trained on each corpus, compared on
each test domain. "A beats B" needs at least 4 of 5 arrays and at least 10 %
mean reduction.
- **Generated test:** generated-trained beats WikiText-trained at every rate,
  5/5 arrays.
  - Standard: 34–50 % lower KL.
  - Corruption-aware: 57–68 % lower KL.
- **WikiText test** ([exp-002](exp-002.md)): WikiText-trained beats
  generated-trained at every rate, 5/5.
  - Standard: 36–150 % lower KL.
  - Corruption-aware: 73–88 % lower KL.
- **Verdict: specialization.** Each corpus wins on its own domain, everywhere.

**Within each domain: corruption-aware against standard HWA** (secondary,
same rule):
- **In-domain, the crossover is at about 3 % for both corpora.**
  - WikiText on WikiText (Experiment 1): standard wins at 0–1 %, ties at 2 %,
    and corruption-aware wins at 3–5 % (18–38 %).
  - Generated on generated: standard wins at 0 % (37 %), ties at 1–2 %, and
    corruption-aware wins at 3–5 % (20–34 %).
- **Out of domain, corruption-aware HWA largely loses its advantage.**
  - Generated sources on WikiText: corruption-aware wins only at 4–5 %
    (14–22 %).
  - WikiText sources on generated text: corruption-aware never wins. It
    loses at 0–4 % (13–114 % higher KL) and ties at 5 %.

**Out-of-domain penalty** (out-of-domain KL ÷ in-domain KL at 0 %):

## Interpretation

**Observations.**
- HWA distillation specializes the analog MLP weights to the training text
  distribution. Out of domain, KL on healthy hardware is 2–3× the in-domain
  value, at every fault rate, for both corpora.
- The large "data effect" of Experiment 1b is this specialization. Neither
  corpus is better in general on this evidence.
- Corruption-aware HWA's high-fault-rate advantage is real in-domain, with a
  crossover at about 3 % for both corpora. It transfers worse than standard
  HWA's robustness.

**Inference.**
- Experiment 1b and 1c's L6/L3 generated-text arms were all scored out of
  domain on WikiText. Their source rankings describe out-of-domain
  robustness. There, stronger generic noise (6–12 %) matched or beat
  corruption-aware training. That need not hold in-domain:
  - in-domain, corruption-aware HWA beats 3 % standard HWA by 20–34 % at
    3–5 %;
  - the noise arms were not evaluated in-domain.
- The generated-text sources pay a smaller out-of-domain penalty (1.9–2.0×
  against 2.6–2.8×). This is consistent with less overfitting: 2.1 passes
  over 50 M tokens, against 50 passes over 2.1 M. It is one training run per
  cell.

**Limits.**
- The generated test has 384 sequences (98 K scored tokens) and comes from
  the same truncated-sampling generator as the training text.
- WikiText-trained `standard_hwa` was trained on a 3090 for the WikiText
  cell and on a 5090 for this one.
- Constant learning rate throughout; [exp-003](exp-003.md) part B showed
  that decay changes absolute values by 11–27 %.
- Exploratory, model-based evidence.

## Decision

**Pending your decision:**
1. **The deployment domain.** Recovery experiments must declare their test
   domain, and should report both domains. A source's fault robustness
   cannot be read across domains.
2. **The HWA training corpus.** One option is to match it to the declared
   domain. Another is to train on a mixture (WikiText-2 train plus generated
   text) and evaluate on both.
3. **For on-chip recovery.** In-domain adaptation data could recover the
   domain gap as well as the fault damage. Experiment 2 needs a
   matched-domain digital control (calibration or rewrite on the same data)
   to separate the two.
