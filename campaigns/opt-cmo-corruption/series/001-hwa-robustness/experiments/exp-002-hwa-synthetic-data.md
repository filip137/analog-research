# exp-002: Experiment 1b, HWA on teacher-generated text (L6)

## Question and dependencies

**Question.** OPT-125M runs with CMO fc1/fc2 in its last six decoder layers.
Each HWA source is now distilled on 50 M tokens of teacher-generated text
instead of 50 passes over WikiText-2. Each is deployed without retraining on
5 independent arrays at 0–5 % random stuck devices. Three sub-questions:

1. Do Experiment 1's conclusions hold with broad training data? These are:
   HWA is essential, and standard and corruption-aware HWA cross over at
   about 2–3 %.
2. Does stronger generic Gaussian noise in standard HWA (6 % instead of 3 %)
   buy robustness to stuck devices, and at what cost on healthy hardware?
3. What does overestimating the fault rate in corruption-aware HWA cost?
   Training at 5 % stuck devices is compared with 3 %; the cost of interest
   is at deployed rates below 5 %.

**Decision informed.** Which sources to carry into on-chip recovery, now
Experiment 2. Also whether HWA for this campaign should use generated text,
stronger noise or a higher training fault rate.

**Motivation** ([exp-001 result](../results/exp-001.md)).
- **Overfitting.** Experiment 1 repeated 2.1 M WikiText-2 tokens 50 times.
  Its development/training ratio grew in every source, and the test set was
  in-distribution.
- **Uneven convergence.** At L6, corruption-aware HWA had plateaued while
  standard HWA was still gaining robustness, so the crossover is conditional
  on the budget.

**Expected outcome (stated before running).**
- With the new data, standard HWA closes part of the gap at 3–5 %.
- 6 % noise improves 1–5 % robustness and costs fidelity at 0 %.
- Corruption-aware HWA at 5 % costs more at 0–1 % than at 3 % and gains at
  4–5 %.

**Dependencies.**
- The [exp-001 execution contract](exp-001-hwa-source-robustness.md), reused
  unchanged except where stated here.
- The new `hwa.sequences_per_epoch` contract extension:
  [lifecycle contract](../../../../../docs/crossbar_lifecycle.md), tested in
  `tests/test_workflow_cmo.py`.

## Metrics and decision

**Unit, metric and aggregation** are as in Experiment 1: per assignment ×
stuck rate; deployed teacher KL on the test cohort; perplexity also
reported; never pooled across rates.

**Decision rule (frozen before results).** "A beats B at p" uses
Experiment 1's rule: lower KL on at least 4 of 5 arrays and a mean relative
reduction of at least 10 %. It is applied to these comparisons:

1. **Data effect.** `standard_hwa` and `cdt_random_3pct` against their
   Experiment 1 L6 counterparts. The arrays and test tokens are the same, so
   the comparison is paired per array. The Experiment 1 L6 `standard_hwa`
   trained in another environment (recorded deviation).
2. **Stronger noise.** `standard_hwa_6pct` against `standard_hwa`, at every
   rate.
3. **Overestimation.** `cdt_random_5pct` against `cdt_random_3pct`, at every
   rate. Its cost is the rates where 5 % loses, expected at 0–3 %.
4. **Per-rate best set.** At each rate, report the source with the lowest
   mean KL, plus every source it does not beat. This replaces Experiment 1's
   carry-forward rule, which returns nothing under a crossover.

   Proposed carry-forward: the union of the best sets at 1 % and at 3–5 %,
   capped at the two sources that cover most rates.

**Validity guards.**
- The `prepare` gate: digital KL ≤ 1e-6. No nonfinite values.
- **Reproduction check.** `pretrained` deployments must reproduce Experiment 1
  L6 `pretrained` KL to a relative 1e-6: the same platform, arrays and test
  tokens. Investigate any mismatch before interpreting.
- **Convergence.** Report each trained source's:
  - selected epoch;
  - 40→50 change of the healthy and 3 % components;
  - development/training ratio at epochs 10 and 50.

  A fixed budget is reported as such.
- Fewer than 5 complete assignments for any source is inconclusive coverage.
