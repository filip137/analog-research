# Result: exp-008, dense on-chip pilot

The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-008-overnight-recovery.md) owns the
frozen comparison. **Scientific coverage is pending.** Readiness measurements
below are not evidence for recovery from the frozen six-MLP HWA source.

The one-MLP smoke completed on loulou in 17.30 seconds of native campaign time,
20.49 seconds including queue transport and declared validation. It used
4,718,592 analog weights, a pretrained teacher source, 64 training sequences,
two optimizer steps, 3% permanent random faults, and matched hold/learn arms.
Both final states replayed exactly. The learning arm recorded 9,155,042 healthy
active-device writes, 282,142 stuck-write attempts, 4,577,521 touched devices,
maximum two writes per device, and zero verify reads. Peak CUDA reserved memory
was 13,899,923,456 bytes (12.95 GiB). There were 2,347 out-of-range devices at the
endpoint; unclamped CMO write noise permits this and it must remain visible.

The canary is numerically valid but **worse at this short endpoint**: generated
teacher KL rose from 0.13378 to 0.15397, development KL from 0.13669 to 0.16418,
and WikiText perplexity from 57.06 to 62.26. It used 7,027,819,604 healthy-device
writes and 217,343,404 stuck attempts; 27,454,801 devices were touched, with a
maximum of 256 writes/device and zero verify reads. Out-of-range devices reached
1,767,475. These observations are consistent with dense write noise imposing a
cost, but do not isolate that mechanism. The predeclared full pass remains the
duration test; no LR, schedule, source or selection rule was changed in response
to this endpoint. The 5e-3 LR remains a chosen pilot setting, not a tuned
on-chip optimum. Sparse-write experiments and broader control comparisons await
review of the baseline.
