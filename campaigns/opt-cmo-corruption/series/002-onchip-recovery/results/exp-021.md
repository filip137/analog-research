# Result: on-chip learning-rate and schedule screen

**For exact digital Adam, the healthy-array degradation was a learning-rate
effect.**
- Cosine decay at peak 3e-3 makes it healthy-safe (development KL 0.0191 vs 0.0208
  held).
- It is also the best 3% endpoint in the screen (0.0616).

**For the physical noisy thresholded law, no tested recipe has both.**
- Constant 1e-3 is healthy-safe, and even improves the healthy array (0.0193).
  At 3% it recovers less than the frozen constant 3e-3 (0.0775 vs 0.0671).
- Cosine decay hurts this law at every peak rate.

Under the frozen rule, the noisy recipe is **not replaced**. The exact-write
oracle is **valid** at cosine 3e-3. The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-021-onchip-lr-screen.md)
owns the frozen comparison and rule.

**Frozen decisions.**
- **Noisy law:** the selected configuration is constant 1e-3, which is
  healthy-safe. Its 3% KL is 0.0775, above 0.0671 for constant 3e-3, so it is
  **not adopted**. The frozen exp-013 recipe stays for follow-ups.

  and not rising between updates 192 and 256 in either case, so it is a **valid
  oracle**.

## Interpretation

**Observed.**
- With exact writes, every rate of 1e-3 or below, and cosine at 3e-3, improves
  the healthy array. Learning on a healthy array is not inherently harmful: the
  exp-015 degradation came from constant 3e-3.
- With noisy thresholded writes:
  - a lower constant rate trades healthy safety for recovery;
  - cosine is worse than constant at every peak rate, in both cases;
  - every noisy trajectory is still flat or falling at update 256.

**Inferred.**
- **Why cosine hurts the noisy law:** the thresholded writer issues a write only
  once an accumulated command exceeds 2σ of programming noise (about 0.028 q). A
  decaying rate shrinks commands below that threshold, so late updates are mostly
  never written. Cosine 3e-3 issues 23M writes against 36M at constant 3e-3.
- **The digital schedule doesn't transfer.** The fine late-phase adjustments that
  help digital Adam cannot be expressed in the physical array.
- **Revision of exp-015.** That experiment reported write noise and gating as a
  small share, but its comparison was against a mistuned digital Adam. Against
  the tuned oracle, the physical gap is material: 9% of the 3% development KL
  for the frozen recipe, 26% for the healthy-safe recipe. Write precision and
  gating, rather than optimizer noise, now look like the limit at 3% random
  faults.
- **Budget.** The flat or falling ends suggest the lower-rate noisy recipes are
  also budget-limited. A longer run at 1e-3 might close part of the gap. This
  is untested.

**Limits.**
- One array, one HWA source and one seed. Only healthy and 3% random faults; no
  rewrite and no gmin/gmax faults.
- Selection uses 256 development sequences. Differences of a few percent are
  within unmeasured run-to-run noise.
- The cosine warm-up and floor were not varied.

## Next decision

Nothing was launched. Options for the user:
1. Keep the frozen recipe for the rewrite + learning and calibration comparison.
   This screen does not invalidate it at 3%. Its healthy degradation is a known
   cost.
2. Test recipes that respect the write threshold:
   - a constant rate with a step decay that stops writing rather than shrinking
     commands;
   - a decaying rate with a matched lower threshold;
   - a longer budget at 1e-3.
3. Repeat the selected exact and noisy rows on the rewritten gmin/gmax arrays of
   exp-016. The learnable residual there differs from random faults.
