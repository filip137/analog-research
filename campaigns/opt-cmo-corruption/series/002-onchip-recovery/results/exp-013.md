# Result: recovery across HWA and corruption regimes

**Complete and reviewed October 13:** all 27 recovery jobs, 54 starting conditions
and 162 recovery endpoints passed native validation and local collection. All
297 collected compact-file hashes were verified. The last worker finished on
October 12 at 22:22 Europe/Paris; final collection completed by 22:24. There were
28 successful attempts including preparation, with no execution retries,
exclusions or replacement arrays. The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-013-recovery-regimes.md)
owns the frozen design. No prospective hypothesis IDs were assigned; the scoped
review and decision are recorded below.

Six analog OPT-125M MLPs contain **28,311,552 logical weights**. Each recovery uses
2,097,152 tokens, 256 updates, LR 0.003, signed accumulation and a nominal 2-sigma
write threshold. Caps count issued attempts per active device, including stuck
attempts, and exclude initial programming. Digital parameters and reference
cells stay fixed. All arms use their named held deployment and the final
checkpoint. Assignments 271002–271004 are independent arrays, not three HWA
training seeds.

Recovery from either standard Gaussian HWA source also beats the best of the
three HWA-only sources on the same array at every 1–5% fault rate, on both
held-out domains, for every cap. Recovery from fault-aware HWA beats that best
HWA-only reference at 3–5% on WikiText and 2–5% on generated text, but does not
at the lower rates. Improvement over its own deployment alone is therefore not
enough to establish the best available endpoint.

Healthy arrays require separate interpretation. Standard-3% HWA worsens on all
three arrays and all caps in both held-out domains. Standard-9% HWA worsens at
cap 4 and uncapped; cap 2 slightly improves WikiText (mean 0.515%, all three
arrays), but generated-test signs are mixed (one of three improves). Fault-aware
HWA improves its own healthy endpoint, yet remains worse than healthy standard-3%
HWA without recovery. No recovery arm beats the best healthy HWA-only endpoint.

These healthy-array statements concern teacher KL. Perplexity changes have the
same sign as KL changes for every endpoint at 1–5% faults, but 20 of 81 healthy
endpoints disagree. WikiText perplexity improves for standard-3% HWA on 7 of 9
endpoints (mean change −0.05 to −0.11 by cap) and for standard-9% HWA on all
nine (−0.40 to −0.55), although teacher KL worsens on all of them except
standard-9% cap 2. The other seven disagreements are standard-9% development
and generated-test endpoints with changes near zero. Teacher KL is the recovery
objective and remains the comparison metric; these small perplexity gains do not
establish healthy-chip benefit.

At 3% actual faults, mean WikiText perplexity changes from **57.12 to 53.13**
for standard-3% HWA, **55.35 to 54.36** for fault-aware HWA, and **54.81 to 53.03**
for standard-9% HWA with cap 4. KL reduction percentages are not perplexity or
accuracy improvement percentages.

### Teacher, deployment and additional writes

The matched FP32 teacher has PPL **46.601966** on WikiText, **7.125089** on
generated test and **6.937890** on development. This reference comes from the
clean digital `pretrained` arm of exp-1e, whose method is `none` and fit kind is
`digital_teacher`. All three splits reproduce teacher predictions exactly with
absolute KL below 3e-9. The pinned teacher hash and full cohort metadata match
the exp-013 recovery cache and all three source preparation records. These are
cohort-specific measurements, not a published whole-dataset teacher score.
`reviews/teacher-reference.json` records the inputs, hashes and verification.

maximum successful writes is 11–13 at 3% faults, depending on source and array.
Recovery does not close the remaining gap to the teacher.

## How much recovery survives the write caps

Retained improvement is `(deployment KL - capped KL) / (deployment KL - uncapped KL)`.
A nonpositive uncapped gain gives null, not a retention percentage. Values above
100% are preserved: changing the cap changes the trajectory and can improve the
endpoint. These percentages concern this frozen short-run reference, not all
performance lost at deployment. At **3% actual faults**, WikiText retention is:

| HWA source | Cap 2, mean (range across arrays) | Cap 4, mean (range across arrays) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard 3% Gaussian | 93.85% (93.67–94.09%) | 99.70% (99.36–99.98%) |
| Fault-aware 3% | 104.23% (99.53–109.93%) | 103.00% (99.90–106.83%) |
| Standard 9% Gaussian | 97.45% (96.86–97.84%) | 100.16% (99.94–100.30%) |

For standard-3% HWA, cap-2 mean retention at each of 1%, 2%, 3%, 4% and 5%
actual faults is **89.28%, 94.33%, 93.85%, 93.15%, 94.23%**; cap-4 mean retention
is **98.01%, 100.07%, 99.70%, 99.60%, 99.93%**, respectively. The individual-array
minimum is 82.54% for cap 2 at 1% faults, and 96.82% for cap 4 at 1% faults.
For fault-aware and standard-9% HWA, cap 2 sometimes exceeds uncapped gain at low
rates. All individual values, means and ranges remain separate by source, rate,
cap and domain in the linked machine-readable evidence.

## Interpretation and decision

The frozen recipe recovers part of corruption-induced degradation across all
three independent arrays and all tested nonzero fault rates. Four attempted
writes per device preserve nearly all of the corresponding uncapped gain; two
offer a larger maximum-wear reduction at a source- and rate-dependent cost.
This extends the earlier single-array result. It does not establish universal
healthy-chip benefit, optimized per-regime training, or sparse total device use.

This comparison is complete; no additional run, LR change or source/cap selection
is authorized by its descriptive results. The remaining scientific question is
whether these gains beat matched frozen-target rewriting and digital calibration
at declared data and physical budgets. Those controls were not tested here.
One HWA training seed per source, reused evaluation cohorts, three arrays,
homogeneous synthetic faults, short adaptation and idealized unclamped writes
limit generalization. The evidence is exploratory and model-based, not a claim
of demonstrated fabricated-hardware viability.
