# Result: recovery context length after HWA

**Use recovery context 256 for this tested regime.** It is the shortest screened
context meeting the predeclared 95%-gain rule under two, four and uncapped write
attempts. Against the same-budget context-1,024 reference, it passed confirmation
on all three additional arrays at both fixed evaluation contexts. Four attempts
per device give nearly the same recovery as uncapped updates; two attempts save
more total writes with a modest reduction in recovery.

This is an exploratory, model-based result for frozen standard-3% Gaussian HWA,
3% permanent random CMO faults, and the last six OPT-125M MLPs: 28,311,552 logical
weights and 56,623,104 physical devices. It establishes sufficiency under the
frozen rule in this setup, not a universal optimum or fabricated-hardware result.
The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-014-context-length.md) owns the protocol.

## Context selection and confirmation

The primary metric is teacher KL on development data at **fixed evaluation
context 1,024**, where the screening HWA-only baseline was **0.148204**. Lower
is better. Recovery context is the intervention; evaluation context is held fixed.

| Recovery context | Cap two | Cap four | Uncapped |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 64 | 0.087227 | 0.083720 | 0.083395 |
| 128 | 0.083359 | 0.080140 | 0.080072 |
| 256 | 0.081339 | 0.078158 | 0.077926 |
| 512 | 0.078048 | 0.074868 | 0.074751 |
| 1,024 | 0.077849 | 0.075096 | 0.074860 |

Contexts 64 and 128 fail the frozen retention rule. Context 256 meets it for all
three budgets at both evaluation contexts; at evaluation context 256 it is the
best screen point for each budget. Context 512 captures nearly all of the primary
improvement, with little or no additional primary improvement from 1,024 in this
screen. Only the selected 256-versus-1,024 pair was independently confirmed;
512 was not a confirmation arm.

For confirmation, retained gain is `(K0 - K256) / (K0 - K1024)`, with each pair
sharing its array-specific deployment. Both gains are positive in every pair.
The table lists ranges across the **three separate confirmation assignments**;
no significance claim is made from three arrays.

| Write budget | Gain retained, evaluation 1,024 | Gain retained, evaluation 256 | Context-256 KL reduction from HWA-only, evaluation 1,024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Two attempts | 95.03–95.79% | 103.68–104.41% | 46.44–47.27% |
| Four attempts | 95.10–96.50% | 104.09–105.64% | 48.05–48.99% |
| Uncapped | 95.22–96.41% | 104.11–105.64% | 48.17–49.04% |

All nine budget-by-array comparisons pass at both contexts. Context 1,024 still
has lower primary KL; context 256 meets the declared tolerance and does better
at the secondary evaluation context. The minimum margins above 95% are narrow,
especially 95.03% for cap two and 95.10% for cap four. This supports the frozen
sufficiency criterion, not equality of the two contexts.

## Write cost and reporting-only transfer results

At the same recovery context 256, across the screening array and three
confirmation arrays, cap four retains **99.67–99.90%** of uncapped primary
development gain. Cap two retains **95.14–96.62%**. Cap four reduces total
attempts by only **0.56–0.63%**; its main advantage is limiting the most heavily
written cells. Cap two reduces attempts by **13.32–13.72%**. These comparisons
hold context, data, update count and array fixed.

## Decision and limits

Carry **context 256** into subsequent recovery work using this frozen setup.
A **four-attempt cap** is a reasonable default when the priority is preserving
uncapped recovery while bounding per-cell writes; a two-attempt cap is the
lower-total-write option. This is an interpretation of the completed comparison,
not authorization to launch another experiment.

The evidence covers one HWA recipe/seed, one fault rate and placement scenario,
one adaptation cohort and one endpoint realization per assignment. The three
confirmation arrays were already used in earlier HWA evaluation. The smallest
qualifying length was screened on one array; shorter lengths were not retested
on the confirmation arrays. No global optimum through OPT's 2,048-token limit,
other defect regimes, fresh-state transfer, or general on-chip viability is
established. The broader viability claim still requires matched rewrite and
digital-calibration controls and coverage across fault rates. Further runs would
be a separately specified experiment.
