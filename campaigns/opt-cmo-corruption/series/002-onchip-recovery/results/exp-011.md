# Result: exp-011, noise-referenced thresholded writes

**All four screens validated and collected; the selected full pass is running.**
The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-011-thresholded-writes.md) owns the
frozen comparison, decision rule, budget and handoff. Evidence uses the same
standard 3% Gaussian HWA source, permanent 3% random faults, assignment 271001,
and final six OPT-125M MLPs (28,311,552 logical weights) as exp-010.

## Validated short screens

Each screen uses 2,097,152 synthetic input tokens, 256 optimizer updates and
constant LR 3e-3. The threshold is a multiple of the **nominal mid-range**
write-noise standard deviation, 0.01413954 normalized q units. Actual device
noise remains conductance-dependent. Every row starts from exactly the same P0;
the held development KL is 0.13669190.

The two accumulated variants reduce development KL by 49.19% and 50.24%,
respectively. Their physical write counts are 62.18 and 164.91 times smaller
than the dense reference. The 1-sigma variant touches 27,096,522 healthy cells;
2-sigma touches 22,595,133. Fewer total writes therefore does not mean a similarly
large reduction in the number of distinct cells ever written. Stuck attempts
are 4,056,988 and 1,613,959; verify reads are zero. Final out-of-range counts are
808,373 and 753,984, versus 321,385 initially.

The 3-sigma variant touches 17,415,561 healthy cells, incurs 948,970 stuck
attempts and ends with 714,662 out-of-range devices. Discard-only makes no
physical changes: all endpoints exactly match P0. All costs are simulated
idealized device writes, not measured pulse counts. Fixed-development curves
are saved in `analysis/threshold-screens.{png,svg,csv}` under the evidence root;
the plotting script is alongside them. The dense reference has only its final
fixed-development endpoint, not an interpolated training curve.

Native validation, full artifact inspection, exact saved-state replay, lineage
and compact collection pass for all four new rows. Successful compute plus
validation charged **426.06 GPU-seconds** across loulou and trex. The first measured
reserved CUDA peak is 19.88 GB, within the 26,000 MiB reservation. The collector
projects 35.7–37.0 minutes including margin for a qualifying full pass.

## Interpretation and limits

The short results support testing accumulated writes: useful adaptation is
possible under the same idealized noisy-write law once many small commands are
combined into fewer larger writes. This comparison changes update timing,
magnitude and noise exposure together; it does not isolate their separate
causal contributions. It also does not validate the endpoint-derived incremental
noise law or its unclamped extrapolation.

The complete development-only gate selected **accumulated nominal 2-sigma**, at
LR 3e-3, and released one fresh 49,977,856-token pass. Test metrics did not enter
selection. It is running on loulou in attempt
`a5ab01cb747b4763ba7fc2928494c500`. At the first progress check, update 512 had
development KL 0.058289 and 71,542,384 healthy writes. At update 768, development
KL rose to 0.089373 with 103,591,197 writes, so early improvement is not monotonic.
These are intermediate measurements from the full cohort, not its final endpoint.
The full run uses a
different-size shuffled training cohort from the short screen; intermediate
points are not paired minibatch comparisons between the two runs.

No full-pass or convergence claim follows yet. This is one array and fault case,
with no claim of physical viability beyond the best matched no-weight-learning
alternatives. The discard control shows why simply dropping individual small
commands is insufficient at this LR; signed accumulation permits useful writes.
