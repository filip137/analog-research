# Result: noiseless versus noisy learning after intelligent P&V

**Noiseless learning improves every case.** After intelligent P&V it lowers
WikiText PPL by 0.68–1.32 and removes 53–80% of the remaining WikiText teacher-KL
gap to the healthy deployment, for gmin/gmax at 1–6% and random 3%. It also
improves the healthy array (PPL 49.586 → 48.868).

**Noisy learning, the meeting recipe, helps only at higher rates.** It removes
15–32% of the remaining WikiText KL at 3–6%. It is neutral or harmful at 1–2%,
on random 3% and on the healthy array, where its WikiText KL rises by 15%. Its
WikiText PPL changes are mostly small and mixed, from −0.58 to +0.18.

The gap between the two is therefore large in every case, and is largest at low
fault rates. The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-022-pv-learning-noise.md)
owns the frozen comparison.

**Costs, additional to intelligent P&V.**
- **Noisy learning:** 35.6–37.6M successful device writes, at most 4 attempts
  per active device, no verify reads.
- **Noiseless learning:** dense exact writes to every healthy active device at
  every update, about 7.0–7.2G write events. This is an oracle bookkeeping
  figure, not a physical cost.

## Interpretation

**Observed.**
- Under exact writes, the same objective, data and budget reach much lower KL than
  the physical law, in all 14 cases.
- The noisy law's benefit grows with the fault rate, as in exp-016. At 1% and on
  healthy arrays it adds error.

**Inferred** (consistent with exp-021):
- The physical law's limit is write precision and gating, not the optimizer or
  the data. Below the 2σ threshold, updates are not written; above it, they carry
  noise.
- At low fault rates the leftover error is small, so the physical writes' own
  perturbation outweighs what they correct.
- A noisy write law closer to the noiseless bound is the main lever, for example
  verify-on-write or a lower, noise-matched threshold.

**Limits.**
- One array, one HWA source, one seed. Synthetic faults; read noise and drift
  disabled.
- The noiseless arm is non-physical: dense updates, no attempt cap, a different
  schedule.
- The noisy recipe's LR was not re-tuned for rewritten arrays.
- No digital-calibration control, so this does not show that on-chip learning
  beats calibration.
