# Result: fault-aware partner rewriting at 3% faults

## Fifth phase: learning through the verified partner, array 271002 (October 16)

**Training the reference wherever the verify scan flagged the active device
stuck removes almost all wasted writes, but barely changes the result.**
Against phase 4's original-active learning, it lowers WikiText teacher KL by at
most 0.0006: about 1–2 extra percentage points of the residual removed, against
the 11–32% learning already removes. On generated text the change is at most
0.0002. The positive phase-4 result therefore does not depend on which device
of the pair is trained.

**Coverage and validity.**
- **Endpoints:** std3, array **271002 only**, gmin/gmax at 2/4/6%, and four
  arms from the phase-3 deployment (`db378a16…`): phase 4's `none`,
  `pair_rewrite_verify` and `pair_rewrite_verify_recovery`, plus
  `pair_rewrite_verified_partner_recovery`. That is 24 endpoints, one attempt,
  exit 0.
- **Host:** riri, 01:25–01:44 Paris. Another user's jobs held fifi's GPU from
  01:00, so the cross-host lock let riri claim the run.
- **Checks:**
  - full artifact inspection passed;
  - the run lifecycle equals the versioned partner lifecycle (`7bfdd5f9…`);
  - the deployment, cache, devices and teacher hashes equal phase 4's;
  - every arm replays exactly;
  - both learning arms start from the rewritten state;
  - all three phase-4 arms reproduce phase 4 exactly (state and prediction
    hashes), across hosts.
- **Cost:** 1,156 s; 20 GB of raw runs on riri.
- **Evidence:** `results/lifecycles/opt125m-cmo-l6-exp16-pair-rewrite/kinds-partner-learn-std3/`,
  with `reviews/summary.json` (`c4377950…`).

**Sign rule, single array** (both held-out domains, teacher KL):
- **Partner versus original-active learning:** adds for gmin 2%, 4% and 6% and
  for gmax 6%; mixed for gmax 2% (generated +0.000002); harms for gmax 4%.
- **Partner versus rewrite only:** adds for gmin 2/4/6% and gmax 4/6%; mixed
  for gmax 2%. This is the same pattern as phase 4's original-active learning.
- These are signs on one array, not significance tests. The adds come from
  differences of a few 10⁻⁴ in KL.

- **Stuck attempts:** routing removes 98% of them for gmin and 96% for gmax.
  The rest are faults the verify scan missed.
- **Routing at gmin 6%:** 1,570,910 pairs route to the reference and 99,975
  pairs (both flagged) are blocked.
- **Distinct devices touched:** rise by 0.36–1.13M.
- **Other costs:** no verify reads beyond the rewrite's one scan. The role map
  takes 28.3 MB.

**Interpretation.**
- **Observed:** opening the reference to learning turns 0.74–2.31M futile
  attempts into real reference writes. Teacher KL moves by under 2% of the
  residual. This matches exp-017 at random 3%, where routing cut stuck attempts
  by 93% with no KL gain.
- **Inferred:** the pairs with a stuck active device are only 2–6% of weights.
  Their leftover error is mostly the clipped wrong-sign case, whose reference is
  already at its conductance bound, so neither device can move the weight the
  right way. What learning removes is compensation spread over the healthy
  weights, which both arms train identically.
- **Decision:** which device is trained is not the limiting factor. Routing
  stays an optional cost saving, not a new regime. The calibration-only control
  on the same rewritten arrays remains the next required comparison.

## Fourth phase: on-chip learning on top of the rewrite, array 271002 (October 15)

**On one array, the frozen on-chip learning recipe applied after the verify
rewrite lowers teacher KL further for gmin/gmax faults, and the gain grows with
the fault rate.** At 6% it removes about a third of what the rewrite leaves on
WikiText and about half on generated text, at about 12–34× the rewrite's device
writes. At 2% the gain is small (gmin) or absent (gmax WikiText). There was no
calibration arm, by the user's choice, so this does not show that learning beats
digital calibration.

**Coverage and validity.**
- **Endpoints:** std3, array **271002 only** (the user's one-array
  instruction), gmin/gmax at 2/4/6%, and three arms from one P0 per case:
  `none`, `pair_rewrite_verify` and `pair_rewrite_verify_recovery`.
  That is 6 starting conditions and 18 endpoints, one attempt, exit 0.
- **Checks:**
  - full artifact inspection passed;
  - the run lifecycle equals the versioned learning lifecycle;
  - the on-chip input is the phase-3 deployment of 271002 (`db378a16…`);
  - every arm replays exactly;
  - learning starts from the rewritten state;
  - `none` and `pair_rewrite_verify` reproduce phase 3 exactly (state and
    prediction hashes).
- **Cancelled before launch:** the 271003 and 271004 learning runs, by user
  instruction; no attempts were created.
- **Cost:** 684 s on fifi; 13 GB.
- **Evidence:** `results/lifecycles/opt125m-cmo-l6-exp16-pair-rewrite/kinds-learn-std3/`,
  with `reviews/summary.json` (`91786112…`).

**WikiText** (healthy PPL 49.59, KL 0.0443):

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| gmin 4% | 110.18 | 51.20 / 0.0828 | 50.86 / 0.0730 | −0.0098 | 25% |
| gmin 6% | 205.59 | 51.64 / 0.1053 | 51.39 / 0.0858 | −0.0195 | 32% |

| gmax 4% | 103.58 | 50.77 / 0.0827 | 50.95 / 0.0768 | −0.0060 | 16% |
| gmax 6% | 199.36 | 51.68 / 0.1057 | 51.16 / 0.0858 | −0.0199 | 32% |

On generated text (healthy PPL 7.22) learning lowers KL in all six cases. It
removes 31/42/48% of the residual for gmin and 17/36/49% for gmax at 2/4/6%.
Perplexity changes there are small and of mixed sign, from −0.087 to +0.048.

**R6 sign rule, single array** (both held-out domains, teacher KL): learning
**adds** for gmin 2%, 4% and 6% and for gmax 4% and 6%. It is **mixed** for
gmax 2%: WikiText worsens and generated text improves. Teacher KL and
perplexity can disagree: for gmax 4%, WikiText KL improves while perplexity
rises by 0.19.

**Learning cost** (additional to the rewrite):
- 36.4–37.7M successful device writes and 37.3–40.2M issued attempts;
- 0.79–2.55M of those attempts hit stuck devices;
- about 20.5M distinct active devices touched (72%), at most 4 attempts per
  device, no verify reads.

The rewrite itself used 1.09–3.17M device programs plus one scan of
56,623,104 reads. Writes are modeled events, not pulses.

**Interpretation.**
- **Observed:** unlike `random` 3% (phase 1, where learning after the rewrite
  slightly harmed std3 WikiText KL on all three arrays), gmin/gmax residuals
  are partly learnable. The learnable share grows with the fault rate.
- **Inferred mechanism:** the residual is mainly zeroed wrong-sign weights.
  That is structured damage which other weights can partly compensate.
  - At low rates, the recipe's own perturbation, which also degrades healthy
    std3 arrays (exp-013, exp-015), roughly cancels the small gain.
  - The crossover lies between 2% and 4% here.
- **Limits:**
  - one array and one HWA source;
  - a learning rate tuned for unrewritten arrays, never re-tuned;
  - no calibration or off-chip retargeting control.

  It is therefore not established that on-chip learning is the best way to
  remove this residual. The calibration control (no device writes) is the
  required next comparison under AGENTS.md before any viability claim.

rate, from 93% to 95.5%. Rewriting the active device alone becomes harmful from
4% upward.

- gmin: 8.04 → 7.26 at 1%, and 26.69 → 7.52 at 6%;

**Rules:** R1, R2 and R3 pass for every kind × rate. The minimum verify gap
closed is 91.7% (gmax 1%, generated text).

**Observations.**
- **The leftover teacher KL grows almost linearly with the rate.** Above
  healthy, it is 0.009, 0.017, 0.028, 0.039, 0.049 and 0.061 from 1% to 6%
  (WikiText, both kinds). That is about 0.010 per percentage point.
- **Clipped partner targets also scale linearly,** at about 280k per point:
  from about 280k to 1.60M, about half of the 0.55–3.17M programs. Pairs with
  both devices stuck grow quadratically, from about 2.8k to about 101k.
- **Active-only rewriting stops helping at 4%.** It is roughly neutral at 4%
  and catastrophic at 5–6%: WikiText PPL 374 for gmin and 443 for gmax at 6%,
  against about 200 unrewritten.

**Interpretation.**
- **The partner rewrite remains the dominant correction across the whole
  tested range.** The residual it cannot remove grows with the fault rate.
- **The near-linear residual fits mainly the clipped wrong-sign half.** That
  set grows linearly, while both-stuck pairs grow quadratically. This is
  inference; the shares are not separated.
- **Active-only rewriting is harmful,** as the desk estimate predicted. Fixing
  only one role removes the partial cancellation between opposite-sign
  stuck-active and stuck-reference errors.
- **Limits:** one HWA source, synthetic homogeneous faults, no read noise or
  drift, and fitted P&V endpoints rather than pulses.

## Second phase: std3 with gmin, gmax and open at 3% (October 15)

**Stuck-at-Gmin and stuck-at-Gmax faults are far more damaging than random
ones, and the verify-based partner rewrite still removes about 94% of the
damage.** On WikiText, perplexity goes from 49.5 healthy to 80–82 with 3%
faulty devices, then back to 50.4 after the rewrite. The remaining loss is
about three times that of `random` faults, and it comes from the predicted
wrong-sign half. The [experiment note](../experiments/exp-016-pair-rewrite.md)
records the assignment and the operational deviation.

**Coverage and validity.**
- **Endpoints:** the std3 source, arrays 271002–271004, cases gmin, gmax and
  open at 3% per physical device, and the four frozen held arms: 9 starting
  conditions and 36 endpoints.
- **Runs:**
  - all three deployments and on-chip stages are complete;
  - `ebl runs inspect --verify-artifacts --require-complete` passed with full
    artifact hashes;
  - the frozen helper's `checked_report` validated every slice, including the
    shared P0 per array, verify-read counts and held arms unchanged after
    rewriting;
  - each on-chip input is its own array's deployment checkpoint.
- **Matched arrays:** `check-realization` confirmed on all three arrays that
  every healthy device (54.9M) equals the exp-013 random-3% P0. The kinds
  therefore differ from `random` only in stuck values; fault positions and
  healthy programming are shared.
- **Healthy and random references:** taken from the phase-1 rows of the same
  arrays.
- **Execution:** worker `b0c1f06` on loulou (RTX 5090).
  - Wall time: about 13 minutes. Each on-chip stage took about 215 s, and
    each deployment's own stage time is 35 s.
  - Under 0.3 GPU-hours including the readiness checks, against a cap of 1.
  - Storage: 22 GB on loulou.
  - No retries and no failed attempts.
- **Evidence:** `results/lifecycles/opt125m-cmo-l6-exp16-pair-rewrite/kinds-std3/`.

  log and collection/report scripts) and `reviews/summary.json` (`d39fda74…`).

- gmin: 11.05 → 7.36;
- gmax: 10.90 → 7.35;

The oracle pair rewrite equals the verify rewrite to within 0.01 PPL in

**Frozen rules (per kind, all three arrays, both held-out domains):**
- **R1** (verify closes ≥ 90%): gmin, gmax and open all pass. The minimum
  closure is 93.8%, 93.6% and 92.8%.
- **R2** (verify within 2 points of the oracle): all pass.
- **R3** (role swap adds ≥ 20 points): all pass by a wide margin. Active-only
  rewriting closes only 0.6–12.6% for gmin/gmax and 20–27% for open.
  - For gmax on 271002 and 271004, active-only rewriting makes WikiText
    perplexity *worse* than no rewrite, although teacher KL improves slightly.

**Rewrite cost and receipts** (per array):
- **Physical scheme:** one verify scan of 56,623,104 devices, then 1.61–1.65M
  device programs. That is the same scale as `random`.
- **Clipped partner targets:** 824k for gmin and 824–826k for gmax, which is
  50% of the programs. That is exactly the predicted wrong-sign half. For open
  it is 1.13M (68%).
- **Both devices stuck:** 25.4–25.6k weights.
- **Detection:** 14.5k missed gmin devices and 36k missed gmax devices,
  stuck within 5σ of their own target; open had no misses. There were 26–42
  false detections. Misses cost no KL relative to the oracle.

**Interpretation.**
- **The P&V scheme is again the dominant correction,** and the result holds
  for all three stuck values. The relative gap closed (93–94%) is almost the
  same as for `random`, but the starting damage is much larger. In perplexity
  it is 4× larger for gmin/gmax and 9× for open; in teacher KL, 3.3× and 5.9×.
- **The absolute residual is not the same.**
  - The rewritten KL exceeds healthy by 0.028 for gmin/gmax, against 0.0097
    for `random`; for open it is 0.061.
  - In perplexity, that is +0.9 and +2.4 points against +0.3.
- **This matches the partner geometry, but the attribution is inference.**
  - For every gmin/gmax fault whose weight has the wrong sign, the partner can
    only bring that weight to zero. About 2.9% of all weights are left at
    zero, against about 1.1% of clipped targets for `random`.
  - For open, 0 µS lies below the 9 µS floor, which leaves an offset.
  - This comparison does not separate the shares of clipping, both-stuck
    pairs and programming noise.
- **What remains open:** this residual is what calibration, learning or
  off-chip retargeting would have to reduce. None of those were tested here.
- **Limits:** these are model-based, exploratory results for one HWA source
  and training seed, with synthetic homogeneous faults. There is no read noise
  or drift. A program is one fitted P&V endpoint, not a pulse count. cdt3/std9
  and other rates remain deferred.

## First phase: healthy and random 3%

**The simple rewrite works in this tested regime.** Reading the devices and
reprogramming each detected fault's healthy partner removes 92.5–93.3% of the
fault-caused WikiText teacher-KL increase for standard 3% Gaussian HWA (`std3`).
It needs no training or digital correction parameters. It beats both exp-013
on-chip recovery budgets on every assignment, for every tested source and both
held-out domains. The oracle and measured-map outcomes are nearly identical.

These are exploratory, model-based results for **healthy arrays and 3% random
permanent faults only**. The assigned reduced phase is complete; the broader
rates/kinds comparison remains deferred. The
[experiment note](../experiments/exp-016-pair-rewrite.md) owns the frozen rules.

devices. Rewrite programs touch each selected healthy device once, about 2.71%
of all physical devices. Around half the requests target references; for std3,
active requests range from 766,351–768,827 and reference requests from
767,673–769,691. Request counts include attempts at undetected stuck partners;
those attempts do not change the devices. Oracle pair rewriting programs
1,647,731 healthy devices on average; active-only oracle rewriting programs
824,047. All costs exclude the original deployment.

Subsequent learning additionally issues mean stuck-device attempts of 1.143M,
1.448M and 1.337M for std3/cdt3/std9. Its cap is four attempts per physical
active device, with no verify/retry. Learning remains the existing active-only
update law; it does not acquire a new per-pair update policy after rewriting.
The rewrite is one fitted Gaussian P&V endpoint per request: **programs are not
individual pulses**, and internal P&V pulse/verify costs are not modeled.
Therefore the approximately 27-fold std3 program/write count reduction versus
exp-013 cap four is not an energy or latency ratio.

WikiText perplexities before rewrite / after rewrite / after rewrite plus
learning are 57.119 / 49.818 / 49.510 for std3, 55.354 / 53.765 / 52.997 for
cdt3, and 54.809 / 51.255 / 50.731 for std9. The primary teacher-KL decision
and token perplexity can differ: std3 learning slightly improves perplexity
while worsening agreement with the teacher.

## Detection, residual and frozen decisions

The controller's 5σ detector uses one apparent read and the original target,
without accessing the hidden fault map. It misses approximately 115k–118k
faulty devices per array, yet incurs almost no KL penalty relative to the
oracle. That supports the adequacy of this detector under the declared model;
it does not establish performance with noisy reads. On healthy arrays there
are 26, 28 and 42 false detections, with maximum absolute relative KL change
1.94e-5 across all sources and domains, below the R0 threshold of 1e-4.

About 25.4k–25.6k weights have both devices stuck. Verify-arm target clipping
affects 315k–316k requests for std3, 405k–406k for cdt3 and 362k for std9.
Both-stuck pairs, conductance bounds and programming noise can all contribute
to the residual; this comparison does not separately identify their shares.

- **R1:** std3 and cdt3 pass the 90% gap-closure rule on every array in both
  held-out domains. Std9 fails narrowly: assignment 271002 closes 89.982% on
  WikiText and 89.743% on generated text. Rounding must not turn this into a pass.
- **R2:** every source passes the rule that verify-based closure lies within
  two percentage points of the oracle on every array and domain.
- **R3:** every source passes the role-swap rule. Active-only oracle rewriting
  closes just 38.65–40.43% of std3's WikiText gap; programming healthy references
  when the active is stuck provides a substantial additional benefit.
- **R4:** pair-verify KL is below both exp-013 cap-four and uncapped KL on every
  assignment, for all three sources and both held-out domains.
- **R5:** std3 has the lowest compensated KL on every assignment in both domains.
  Corruption-aware HWA's higher healthy KL remains after compensation.
- **R6:** learning after rewriting helps generated-text KL for all sources.
  On WikiText it helps cdt3 and std9 on every array, while it worsens std3 on
  every array by 0.000010–0.001167 KL. This is the frozen sign rule, not a
  statistical significance claim. Cdt3's closure above 100% means learning
  also improves on its healthy HWA baseline; it is not more than perfect fault
  cancellation.

## Interpretation and next decision

At **3% random faults**, fault-aware pair rewriting is the strongest tested
no-weight-learning control. For the best compensated source, std3, the tested
on-chip recipe supplies no additional WikiText teacher-KL benefit at considerable
write cost. This supports making the simple rewrite the comparison that future
recovery work must beat in this regime. It does not establish that learning is
never useful: the other sources benefit from learning on the residual, though
their final KL remains above std3 plus rewriting alone.

The implementation assumes independently programmable per-weight reference
devices, preserves subtraction polarity, and uses the held post-write state.
Fault rates and stuck values are synthetic; P&V uses the fitted CMO endpoint
model. Read noise, drift, loaded passive-network effects and measured pulse
costs are absent. There is one HWA training seed per source, three independent
array assignments and reused fixed test cohorts. These are simulator results,
not fabricated-hardware deployment measurements.

The assigned implementation and reduced test are complete. The physical ability
to program references and coverage at other fault rates/kinds remain unresolved.
The broader frozen comparison is deferred; this result does not authorize its
launch or a new tuning sweep.
