# Result: exp-019, analog LoRA adapters vs base MLP arrays

**At the same per-device stuck rate, faults in the adapter arrays do not hurt
task accuracy more than faults in the base MLP arrays.** In the raw deployment,
the base arrays are clearly more fragile at 3% for HellaSwag and PIQA, and the
two are comparable for SST-2. After the standing verify partner rewrite, every
loss is at most 0.8 pt and all adapter/base differences are comparable, except
two small PIQA cases. The adapters hold about 1/38 of the devices, so per
*failed device* they are roughly 10× (HellaSwag) to 50× (SST-2) more damaging.

## Interpretation

**Observations:**
- **Base arrays dominate when the adapter is strong.** For HellaSwag the adapter
  carries most of the task gain (about +11 pt development), yet adapter-only
  faults cost 0.3–0.9 pt raw against 1.4–3.8 pt for base-only faults.
- **PIQA's adapter "gains" are noise.** The adapter adds only about 3 pt there.
  Adapter faults change 4–9% of predictions and move accuracy by noise-level
  amounts, up to −0.9 pt, i.e. an apparent improvement.
- **SST-2 is comparable.** Its decision margin is large, and both kinds of
  fault cost 1–2 pt raw.
- **Per device, adapters are far more damaging.** 44k adapter devices do
  10–50× more damage per device than the 1.7M base devices.
- **The verify rewrite neutralises both.** At ≤3% faults, losses are at most
  0.8 pt.

**Inference:** at equal *rates*, adapters are not the weak point. The base
holds about 38× more faulty devices, which outweighs the adapters' higher
per-device leverage. Placing adapters on separate small arrays does not need
extra protection beyond the standing rewrite, in this regime.

**Limits:**
- one array, one HWA base, one adapter seed per task, rank 16;
- adapter arrays inherit the same per-device rate (a separate, denser fault
  process for small arrays was not modelled);
- model-based results.
