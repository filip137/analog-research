# Result: exp-020, on-chip recovery of the analog-LoRA deployments

**After the verify partner rewrite there is almost nothing left to recover, even
at 6% stuck devices.** Rewrite alone (R1) brings every case to within about
1 pt of the clean adapted model. For example, HellaSwag at gmax 6% goes from
32.3% raw to 41.3% after the rewrite, against 42.1% clean.

On top of the rewrite:
- **Digital calibration (R2) and adapter-only on-chip learning (RA):** no
  detectable test-accuracy effect in any case. |Δ| ≤ 0.6 pt, all within about
  2 SE, with two small exceptions below.
- **Both do pull predictions toward the clean model.** Agreement with it rises
  by 1–4 pt at 6%. Because about as many flipped predictions become wrong as
  become right, accuracy does not move.
- **All-weight on-chip learning (RW) hurts.** It costs up to −2.9 pt on
  HellaSwag and collapses PIQA to about 53.6%, near chance, in the gmax and
  adapter-fault cases. It writes 25–70× more than RA.

## Interpretation

**Observations:**
- **The rewrite does the recovery.** At ≤6% faults, the verify partner rewrite
  removes nearly all accuracy loss. This holds for base, adapter and all-array
  faults alike (exp-019 at 3%; here up to 6%).
- **Small analog adapters are a safe recovery handle, but not a useful one in
  accuracy here.** Adapter-only learning writes only adapter devices (0.7–2.1M
  attempts, never the base). It nudges predictions back toward the clean model
  (+1–3 pt agreement at 6%), and test accuracy is unchanged. Calibration does
  the same with no device writes at all.
- **Whole-array on-chip learning is harmful.** This is consistent with the
  pretrained-LM results (exp-013/016/017): the noisy thresholded writes damage
  the network more than they repair it, catastrophically for PIQA.

**Inference:** in this regime, on-chip training is not a viable
post-deployment strategy beyond the rewrite. The pretrained, fine-tuned and
analog-LoRA settings all show this. Adapter-only learning is the only on-chip
variant that does no harm, at about 1/40 of the whole-array write cost, but it
does not beat zero-write calibration.

**Limits:**
- one array, one source per task;
- ≤6% synthetic, homogeneous faults;
- one on-chip law, four epochs, LRs from a three-point screen;
- the objective is KL to the clean deployed model;
- the screen shared the evaluation array (development data only);
- model-based results.

## Decision

The on-chip learning question for OPT-125M at ≤6% faults with the rewrite is
answered negatively across three regimes. Further work would need a regime
where the rewrite leaves real residual loss: much higher fault rates, faults the
rewrite cannot cancel (both devices of a pair stuck), drift, or read noise.
None is launched without assignment.
