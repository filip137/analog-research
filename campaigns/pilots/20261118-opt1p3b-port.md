## Question and decision
1. Does the existing `opt_mlp_suffix` pipeline run OPT-1.3b unchanged? OPT-1.3b
   has the same pre-LN layout as OPT-125M; only the shape changes.
2. What are the throughput, peak memory and bundle sizes of whole-model HWA at
   L12 and L24 on one A100 80 GB (Jean Zay `gpu_p5`)?

Decision: the probe numbers set the HWA token budget and GPU-hour request,
and decide whether memory engineering (micro-batches, activation
checkpointing, bf16) or MLP-only HWA scope is needed. MLP-only scope is a
scientific choice for the user.

## Interpretation and next decision
- **Memory:** whole-model FP32 HWA fits one 80 GB A100 at batch 32 × 256 for
  both L12 (64.2 GB) and L24 (69.8 GB). No micro-batching, checkpointing,
  bf16 or MLP-only scope is required on 80 GB GPUs. It would not fit the
  32 GB RTX 5090s or the 32 GB V100s.
- **Throughput:** about 1.3–1.6 k tokens/s. At that rate the arithmetic is close to the
  A100's FP32 peak (about 8 × 1.3 B FLOP per token), so the cost is compute
  bound. PyTorch's default leaves TF32 off; enabling TF32 or bf16 would
  be the main speed lever, but it changes the numerics of the analog MVM
  simulation and is a scientific choice.
- **Projection per HWA source** (FP32, A100): the 125M L6 reference used
  12,800 updates (105 M tokens). The same budget at 1.3b is about **19–23
  A100 GPU-hours** of updates, plus about 2 min per selection point and about
  2.5 min per deployed array. Two sources (L12 and L24) at that budget come to
  roughly 45–50 GPU-hours. A 25 % token budget (3,200 updates) is about
  5–6 GPU-hours per source.
- **Queue fit:** one 105 M-token source does not fit the 2 h dev QoS. It needs
  `qos_gpu_a100-t3` (20 h limit, possibly too short) with resume across jobs, or
  `umg@h100` with `qos_gpu_h100-t4`. H100 FP32 throughput is not measured.
- **Storage:** about 5.3 GB per HWA source and 7–8.5 GB per deployed array.

Next decision (user): HWA token budget and GPU-hour allowance, A100 vs
H100, and whether to allow TF32/bf16. The HWA learning-rate and schedule choice
for 1.3b follows that and is not set by this probe.

### Interpretation
- SDPA attention and fused Adam are nearly exact (4e-4 gradient difference,
  at the level of summation reordering), save 6 GB, and give 4 %.
- **TF32 is the lever: 5.0× (L12) and 4.1× (L24) with SDPA and fused Adam.**
  Its gradient deviation (1.5 %) is about 40× the reordering floor but small
  next to the 3 % per-channel weight noise injected every update, and a short
  trajectory stays within about 1 % of FP32.
- bf16 autocast is slower than TF32 here (the teacher remains FP32) and
  deviates far more (11 % loss at the initial state; the analog weights are
  rounded to 8 mantissa bits in the MVM). Not recommended.
- Exact further saving: the HWA cohort (8,192 sequences) is revisited every
  epoch and the teacher is frozen, so caching its final hidden state (about
  17 GB in host memory) removes the teacher pass: about 20 % more under TF32.
- Projection per source at the 125M reference budget (12,800 updates):
  about 3.6 h (L12) and 4.7 h (L24) of updates under TF32 + SDPA + fused,
  against about 18–19 h in FP32, plus selection points. Each fits one
  `qos_gpu_a100-t3` job (20 h).

### Decision for the user
1. Allow TF32 matmuls for HWA training updates. Proposed: only inside the
   update loop; selection, deployment and all evaluation stay FP32. This is a
   declared lifecycle field, recorded in every source.
2. Adopt SDPA attention and fused Adam as opt-in settings, so historical
   125M replays keep their numerics.
3. Implement the cached teacher hidden state (exact, about 20 %).
